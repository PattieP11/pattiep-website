# Downsizing in Petaluma: Where to Start When You Know It's Time

**The short answer:** start with the house you're in, not the one you'll buy. Learn its condition, decide what's worth fixing, and set a realistic timeline. Then decide what the next home has to have. Sorting belongings, choosing repairs and timing the move all get easier once you know those two things. You don't have to do it all at once, and you don't have to do it alone.

## Why does downsizing feel so hard to start?

Because it isn't one decision. It's a dozen of them, all tangled together: what to keep, what to fix, where to go, when to list, how to time the next purchase. When everything depends on everything else, the easiest choice is to wait another year.

Most people don't need more information. They need an order of operations. Once you know what comes first, each step is manageable.

## What's the very first step?

**Walk through your home with someone who knows what buyers will notice.**

Not to list it. Just to learn where you stand. On a first walkthrough, I look at the home's condition room by room: what's in good shape, what's worn, what a buyer's inspector is likely to flag, and what can be left alone.

You come away with a clear picture instead of a pile of worries. That picture is what every other decision rests on.

## Should I get an inspection before I sell?

I recommend it, and I do it for every home I list. It's called a pre-listing inspection: a licensed inspector goes through your home before it goes on the market, the same way a buyer's inspector would later.

It turns surprises into choices. If the inspector finds something, you decide what to do about it on your schedule, with time to get fair bids, instead of negotiating under pressure in the middle of an escrow.

## What should I fix and what should I leave?

Not everything is worth fixing. A good plan sorts the list into three groups:

1. **Fix it.** Safety items, anything that would worry a buyer's lender, and things that are cheap to fix and expensive to leave, like a leak or a broken window.
2. **Refresh it.** Paint, lighting, hardware, a deep clean, a tidy yard. These change how a home feels in photos and showings.
3. **Leave it.** Big remodels usually aren't worth starting right before a sale. Buyers may want to choose those finishes themselves.

I write the plan down for every seller, so you can see the whole list, the order, and what's worth your money.

## How do I deal with years of belongings?

One room at a time, starting with the rooms you use least.

- **Start small.** A closet or a guest room, not the garage.
- **Use three piles:** keep, give to family or friends, donate or sell.
- **Measure your next home's rooms** before you decide which furniture comes with you. Knowing the size of the new space makes the choices clearer.
- **Set a deadline that matches your timeline,** not an arbitrary one.
- **Get help.** Family, a professional organizer or a move manager can take a lot of weight off. I can point you to local help.

You don't have to empty the house before it's listed. You do want it clear enough that buyers can picture themselves in it.

## How do I figure out what my next home should be?

Before you look at a single listing, write down what the next home has to have. Be specific:

- One level, or a bedroom on the main floor?
- How much yard do you want to take care of?
- How many bedrooms do you really use?
- What do you want to be close to?
- Do you need space for guests, a hobby or an office?

A short, clear list saves weeks of touring homes that were never going to work.

## How do I time the sale and the next purchase?

This is the question most people worry about most, so I wrote a full guide to it: [Sell First or Buy First? How to Time a Downsizing Move in Petaluma](sell-first-or-buy-first-petaluma.html).

The short version: decide whether you'll sell first or buy first, plan for the gap between, and put both transactions on one calendar. When your home is already inspected, planned and prepared, you have far more control over the timing.

## What mistakes should I avoid?

- **Starting with the next house.** Falling for a home before you know your current home's condition, value and timeline leads to rushed choices.
- **Trying to sort everything before talking to anyone.** You'll stall. Get the plan first, then sort with a deadline.
- **Starting a big remodel to sell.** Most of the time, a refresh beats a renovation.
- **Skipping the inspection.** Problems found by a buyer's inspector cost more time and money than problems you find first.
- **Forgetting the tax side.** Selling a home you've owned for a long time can have tax effects. Talk to your CPA or tax advisor before you list.
- **Carrying it all yourself.** Downsizing is a lot of small jobs. Hand off the ones you don't have to do.

## What does this look like in real life?

Here's how it can go. This is an example to show the process, not a real client.

An owner has lived in a two-story Petaluma home for years and wants something on one level. They've been "about to start" for a while. On the first walkthrough, we go room by room and set aside the things that don't matter. A pre-listing inspection turns up a few repairs. The plan says fix those, paint two rooms, update the lighting, leave the kitchen alone. A schedule puts the launch date eight weeks out, which gives them time to sort one room a week. With the plan on paper, they write down what the next home needs, and the search finally feels possible.

That's the whole idea: one clear next step at a time.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the first step in downsizing?**
Learn where your current home stands. A walkthrough and a pre-listing inspection show you what's worth fixing, what to leave and how long the prep will take.

**What is a pre-listing inspection?**
A home inspection done before your home goes on the market, by a licensed inspector, the same kind a buyer would order later. It lets you handle problems on your own schedule.

**Should I remodel before I sell?**
Usually not. Repairs, paint, lighting and a deep clean tend to matter more than a big remodel right before a sale.

**Do I have to empty my house before I list it?**
No. It should be clear enough for buyers to picture themselves living there. Sorting one room at a time, on a deadline that fits your timeline, works well.

**How long does it take to get a home ready to sell?**
It depends on the home and the repairs. A written timeline, built after the inspection, gives you a real date instead of a guess.

**Should I sell first or buy first?**
It depends on your money and your timeline. See the full guide: [Sell First or Buy First?](sell-first-or-buy-first-petaluma.html)
